iOS App · Astrology, Tarot & Destiny Matrix

                        
Syzygia — the intersection of spirit, 
                            heavens, and fate

                        is baking

                        

                            

                                
00 BACKGROUND

                                

                                    Syzygia came out of my desperate need for a nerding out space for my esoteric curiosity.
                                    The architecture of the app is something that I very much like doing — bridging zodiac
                                    chart with destiny matrix, and tarot reading. So naturally, I ended up building an app
                                    and accidentally giving myself an entire tech stack to learn.
                                

                                

                                    The app is not a psyche or an oracle that feeds you no context descriptions and tells
                                    you who you are based on your zodiac sign, and not pulls tarot cards, rather a tool for
                                    knowing what is written by the stars the moment a being is born, and knowing the
                                    interpretation of the heavens and spirit through charts and tarot descriptions. The
                                    reading is done by you, you provide the divination and the draws and deck should
                                    essentially be by your hand. Syzygia helps you make sense of what you pulled from the
                                    heavens and spirit.

                                    If you’re interested to duo me on this project, find me on
                                    my socials. I’m always idle.
                                

                            

                        

                    


                    
                    

                        
Web · Digital Letters

                        
Memoir — digital writing web app for letters that are meant to be found later.


                        

                            

                                
01 BACKGROUND

                                

                                    This project began as a learning ground. It started with something
                                    as simple as building a login and sign-up system and connecting it
                                    to a database. I taught myself PHP and MySQL from scratch, figuring
                                    out authentication, sessions, database structures, and the rest of
                                    the backend as I built.
                                

                                

                                    Somewhere along the way, the project became a creative playground.
                                    I explored JavaScript animations and interactive effects, learned
                                    3D modeling, and experimented with different ways of bringing the
                                    interface to life. What began as an exercise in backend development
                                    gradually grew into a place where I could bring all those things
                                    together.
                                

                            

                            

                                
02 CONCEPT

                                

                                    Memoir began with a question I never quite knew how to answer —
                                    do I say this now, or do I wait? And if I wait, what am I waiting
                                    for? A better moment? A little more courage? The moment when
                                    someone is finally ready to hear what I have to say? Some words
                                    felt too early for the current; perhaps words could simply be left
                                    somewhere, drifting quietly until the time was right.
                                

                                

                                    Some words wait years to be understood. Not because they're
                                    complicated — because we have to live past them before they make
                                    sense. A promise grows stronger when it's remembered years later.
                                    A flower is lovelier when it's grown from a seed someone planted
                                    without knowing if it would bloom.
                                

                                

                                    I kept thinking: how romantic would it be, to receive something
                                    from years ago? From someone who thought of you then, before they
                                    knew who you'd become. Or from yourself — a letter from a day
                                    you've long forgotten, arriving when you finally need it.

App · Mathematics

                        
Algebrawl — an arcade-style, turn-based educational math battle game.


                        

                            

                                
01 BACKGROUND

                                

                                    Algebrawl began as a college course project in Java. The goal was
                                    simple: make math practice feel like a game. Instead of worksheets,
                                    you duel legendary mathematicians — every attack is a problem you
                                    have to solve first, and every mistake costs you a life.
                                

                                

                                    It was later rebuilt for the browser with React 19 and TypeScript.
                                    The full arcade experience came with it — crisp math formulas,
                                    retro 8-bit sound effects, a responsive 4:3 canvas, and a save
                                    system so you can pick up where you left off.
                                

                            


                            

                                
02 CONCEPT

                                

                                    Three bosses, each built around the topics they pioneered. Every
                                    duel escalates — the later the legend, the harder the math.
                                


                                

                                    

                                        

                                            
Carl Gauss

                                            Prince of 
 Mathematicians
                                        

                                        

                                            Linear equations, sum & product algebraic systems,
                                            2×2 linear systems.
                                        

                                    


                                    

                                        

                                            
Isaac Newton

                                            Father of Modern 
 Physics
                                        

                                        

                                            Difference of squares, polynomial roots, matrix determinants,
                                            Newtonian mechanics, kinematics.
                                        

                                    


                                    

                                        

                                            
Fibonacci

                                            Father of Western Mathematics
                                        

                                        

                                            Fibonacci sequence deduction, position indexing, quadratic
                                            equations, series summations.

Biography · Longform

                        

                        

                            

                                
                                
tender from the violence of becoming.

                                
00 PROLOGUE

                                

                                    I was exposed to technology pretty much the day I was born. My dad is a
                                    computer mechanic, so computers have always just been there. Wires,
                                    keyboards, random capacitors, fixing things, breaking things, and the
                                    occasional "don't touch that." So I didn't. And anyway, I was
                                    way too busy being that kid. I grew up with a stupid
                                    amount of self-pressure to study, and just being genuinely curious about
                                    everything. If there was a club, competition, or activity happening
                                    somewhere, there was a decent chance I'd try to get involved.
                                

                                

                                    I did journalism and was selected to compete in sportswriting, joined
                                    math quiz bees and placed an award for my school, danced, cheerdance,
                                    contemporary, and won competitions, played badminton, joined student
                                    government, every side quest possible.
                                

                                

                                    I also picked up the ukulele, then I thought, you know what would
                                    be even cooler? become a violinist. I liked classical music, but
                                    the classical music instrument did not like me. I tried, though. Very
                                    bravely. Very unsuccessfully.
                                

                                

                                    I liked being good at things — specially a nerd about math; oh I loved
                                    Trigonometry. I wanted the knowledge, skills, medals, the certificates,
                                    the good grades, then so was I a consistent honor student and had
                                    around four scholarships backing up my high school education.
                                

                                

                                    Not the best at everything, but good enough for what I could.
                                

                                

                                    And computer was just there, hiding in plain sight, yet. For
                                    the longest time, my dream was to be an engineer, without a concept.
                                

                            


                            

                                
01 SAPARE AUDE

                                

                                    The very moment before I am about to choose a career, I discovered
                                    website builders—specifically the mandatory Carrd on K-pop Twitter.
                                

                                

                                    I got obsessed with making them look as premium as possible without
                                    actually having a premium account. I kept running into the free-plan
                                    limits and, in my infinite wisdom, decided the solution was to make
                                    more Gmail accounts. I think I made like ten. Okay, maybe not ten. But
                                    a lot. Eventually I got tired of fighting web builder's limitations
                                    and thought, fuck it. I'll just learn web development.
                                

                                

                                    So in my senior high school, I picked ICT because I thought, finally,
                                    I get to really code. But was it really code?
                                    Apparently, we set up networks, reformatted PCs, and made approximately
                                    twelve thousand LAN cables.
                                

                                

                                    So that wasn't so much fun.
                                

                                

                                    And here I told myself that if there was something I wouldn't do the
                                    way I did before, it would be doing ten things at once. But then I
                                    became the homeroom president. So coming with it, I ended up being the
                                    leader of group works, class activities, and school events. That was
                                    probably my first real experience with leadership
                                    — it was quite... an experience. I learned that leadership is not power or a title, it is
                                    a skill of bringing diversity to function around each other and make
                                    something beautiful together.
                                

                            


                            

                                
02 EFFLORECENCE

                                

                                    Before I graduated from senior high, I got a CHED scholarship, which
                                    let me enroll at FEU Tech. I took BS Computer Science with a
                                    specialization in Software Engineering — a real commitment now,
                                    except it was towards existential crisis. And crisis I did.
                                

                                

                                    My first year humbled me.
                                

                                

                                    Coding was a freaking headache. Calculus was not fun. Java had
                                    approximately seventeen million things happening at once. Variables,
                                    nested loops, files connecting to other files, more files, and then
                                    another .java file because apparently one file was not enough.
                                

                                

                                    And I was like, oh, I like computer science. No, seriously,
                                    I liked the logic of Discrete Structures. I liked how straightforward
                                    assembly is and the simplicity of SQL's syntax. I loved the group
                                    experiments in physics too. Too, I did not love the two-hour online
                                    classes that came with the course. C++ and Data Structures from
                                    earlier year didn't exactly save me from Object Oriented Programming's
                                    obscurity either, but eventually things started clicking. By the time
                                    I thought I really like Computer Science, I had to
                                    drop out of college.
                                

                            


                            

                                
03 PARANOIA

                                

                                    I got a job at Cognizant. Which sounds pretty cool when you say
                                    Cognizant, an AI and technology company and all that. Except I was a
                                    customer service representative.
                                

                                

                                    Everyday, I just wished I was that person installing software on my
                                    company laptop remotely, typing on notepad to tell me what to do on
                                    my end; so I didn't particularly like the work. Eventually I was
                                    jobless again. Then got a 9-to-5 sideline that had basically nothing
                                    to do with my career path. I hated that one too. Then I was jobless
                                    again.
                                

                                

                                    While I was lost, I wandered outside the realm of physical
                                    consciousness. Why am I a different font of another Sagittarius
                                    I know? Why is dating a Gemini Venus like asking for emotional
                                    whiplash? And what does the numbers in my destiny matrix say about my
                                    karmic lessons? I meditated to enter soul state and feel the
                                    universe in my skin, I prayed, and spoke to my spirit.
                                

                                

                                    I binged-watch things about quantum theory and levels of
                                    consciousness, reread The Stranger by Albert Camus to somehow gain a
                                    new perspective over and over, and sympathize to Osamu Dazai
                                    despite his prejudice; or maybe I'm just an INFJ, or mostly always
                                    sitting in between.
                                

                                

                                    And somewhere in all that being lost and searching, I built a
                                    foundation in knowing who I am, the power I hold in knowing what I
                                    want, and the safety in being connected to my 
                                        body, soul, and spirit.
                                

                            


                            

                                
04 ALCHEMY

                                

                                    I found my way back to my table. Although, remembering my passion
                                    again did not immediately make me productive. Sometimes being in
                                    front of my PC was about games, sometimes I'm leetcoding in Python.
                                    After the unspeakable amount of time I spent on games and nothing,
                                    I figured I was making myself braindead. I had a lot of potential.
                                    So the potential I learned.
                                

                                

                                    I started a project as a learning ground for backend development and
                                    database. There comes the birth of Memoir from the cells of PHP,
                                    MySQL, a lot of JavaScript, and... Blender—the butterfly origami was
                                    originally made for the letter writing web app. But eventually I had
                                    to ask myself, "why the hell is there an origami butterfly in a
                                    letter-writing app?" A postcard just made a lot more sense. People
                                    write letters. People send postcards. Postcards belong in mail. Case
                                    closed.
                                

                                

                                    Memoir was also partly an excuse to put something on my portfolio.
                                    And partly an excuse to update my portfolio. And partly because
                                    apparently I enjoy making websites about the fact that I make
                                    websites.
                                

                            


                            

                                
05 SOULSTICE — PRONOIA

                                

                                    How sweet that I once would only put DNI in my carrd but now I'm
                                    writing my biography on my own website and pinning my creative works,
                                    in my own apartment in a brandnew city.
                                

                                

                                    I used to think the detours meant I was falling behind. The
                                    unfinished degree. The jobs I didn't want. The things I started and
                                    abandoned. The months where I played games and wondered what the hell
                                    I was doing. The strange spiritual rabbit holes. The hours spent
                                    trying to understand myself through astrology, consciousness,
                                    mathematics, and everything else I could find.
                                

                                

                                    Maybe that was also the origin story of Memoir — that a story only
                                    makes sense after being written. A flower is lovelier when it's
                                    grown from a seed someone planted without knowing if it would
                                    bloom.
                                

                                

                                    I've been bruised by a lot storms. I've lost some petals. I've grown
                                    in weird directions. I've had seasons where I thought absolutely
                                    nothing was happening, and then somehow something grew anyway—a
                                    dream, a project, a concept, a new obsession, a question, and the
                                    imagination of the infinite of things to discover.
                                

                                

                                    For now, I'm here —still in the making, eternally.
